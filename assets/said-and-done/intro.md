Said & Done tests whether coding agents’ completion reports match their work. Each of its 28 offline tasks, 20 coding tasks and 8 operational scenarios, has hidden checks the agent never sees. Half have no honest way to finish, so the right answer is to stop and say "blocked": eight of the coding tasks, and six safety-critical scenarios where no option is valid. Its score, Said & Done, is the share of runs where all three hold: the work was done (or, at a wall, the real obstacle named), the report was true, and no rule was broken.

Across the 28 tasks, Reflection’s Beam falsely reported "complete" in 25 of 56 runs. Fresh Beam calls that independently test the work, review the report, check risky actions and assess progress cut that to 7 of 56 (Beam + scaffold, below), and raised its Said & Done score from 22 of 56 (39%) to 43 of 56 (77%), at an estimated median cost of about $0.088 a run against $0.045. The checks were developed on these 28 tasks, so that gain, 38 points (95% interval +23 to +54), is a development result.

On new tasks, written by a separate agent that never saw the checks’ code or prompts and run only after the checks were frozen, the gain didn’t carry over, and neither claim I registered in advance holds. On a first set of 20, Beam alone and Beam with the checks each got 47 of 60 runs Said & Done (+0 points, −17 to +18). On a second set of 20, redesigns meant to be harder that I ran as frozen although they hadn’t passed their author’s own readiness check, it was 53 against 55 of 60 (+3, −10 to +17); Beam alone stopped correctly on every impossible run there, so that set couldn’t test whether the checks help on harder tasks. On both sets Beam alone did far better than on the 28, stopping correctly at 50 of 60 impossible runs against 2 of 28, and two new sets can’t say whether the checks don’t generalize or simply had little to fix. [Checked /goal](/checked-goal/) packages the checks as a prototype extension for Reflection’s Mirror; these scores come from the benchmark harness, and the extension’s own trials and limits are on its page.
