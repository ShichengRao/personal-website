Coding agents end their work with a report: a status, "complete" or "blocked", and claims about what they did and how they checked it. Said & Done checks those reports against what actually happened. Each of its 15 offline coding tasks has hidden checks the agent never sees, and three have no honest way to finish, so there the right answer is to stop and say "blocked". A run counts only if the work was done, the report was true, and no rule was broken.

The benchmark centres on Reflection's Beam. On its own, Beam reported "complete" on work that wasn't done in about a quarter of its runs, and on the tasks that can't be finished it never said "blocked". In 46 of its 50 false completions, its own reasoning already showed it knew the goal wasn't met. Telling it to be honest didn't change that.

A second opinion did. Fresh Beam calls that check the work from outside the acting conversation lift its score from about half to over 80% (Beam + scaffold, below). The fix isn't specific to Beam: an earlier version of the same checks lifted DeepSeek V4.1 Flash from 73% to 90%. A version of these checks is packaged as [Checked /goal](/checked-goal/), a prototype extension for Reflection's Mirror.
