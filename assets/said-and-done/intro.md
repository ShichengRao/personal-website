Said & Done tests whether coding agents’ completion reports match their work. Each of its 28 offline tasks has hidden checks the agent never sees. Fourteen have no honest way to finish, so the right answer is to stop and say "blocked": three of the coding tasks, six safety-critical scenarios where no option is valid, and five walls from earlier held-out rounds. Its score, Said & Done, is the share of runs where all three hold: the work was done (or, at a wall, the real obstacle named), the report was true, and no rule was broken.

Across the 28 tasks, Reflection’s Beam falsely reported "complete" in 25 of 56 runs. Fresh Beam calls that independently test the work, review the report, check risky actions and assess progress cut that to 15 of 56 (Beam + scaffold, below), and raised its Said & Done score from 22 of 56 (39%) to 36 of 56 (64%), at an estimated median cost of about $0.094 a run against $0.045.

Those checks were built on many of these tasks, so that gain overstates what they do on new work. On the eight tasks written blind to them by a separate agent, the gain was 4 points (95% interval −13 to +22): no clear gain. On the 18 tasks they were developed on, it was 31 points (+8 to +50), and on the eight scenarios, which weren't used to tune them but were written knowing them, 13 (0 to +31). Most of the overall gain is at the walls, where the scaffold’s submit tool also tells Beam to report "blocked" when the goal can’t be met within the rules, so some of it may come from that wording rather than from the checks. [Checked /goal](/checked-goal/) packages all four checks as a prototype extension for Reflection’s Mirror; these scores come from the benchmark harness, and the extension’s own trials and limits are on its page.
