Said & Done tests whether coding agents’ completion reports match their work. Each of its 28 offline tasks has hidden checks the agent never sees. Fourteen have no honest way to finish, so the right answer is to stop and say "blocked": three of the coding tasks, six safety-critical scenarios where no option is valid, and five held-out walls. A run counts only if the work was done (or the real obstacle identified), the report was true, and no rule was broken.

Across the 28 tasks, Reflection’s Beam falsely reported "complete" in 25 of 56 runs. Fresh Beam calls that independently test the work, review the report, check risky actions and assess progress cut that to 15 of 56 (Beam + scaffold, below). Its Said & Done score rose from 22 of 56 (39%) to 36 of 56 (64%), using the same model for every check: a gain of 25 points (95% interval +9 to +39). Most of it is at the walls, where it scored 12 of its 28 wall runs against Beam alone’s 2; on the tasks that can be done, the gain is within noise (24 of 28 against 20). The scaffold’s submit tool also tells Beam to report "blocked" when the goal can’t be met within the rules, and some of the gain at the walls may come from that wording rather than from the checks. The estimated median cost rose from about $0.045 to $0.094 a run.

The checks were developed on the 15 coding tasks. On eight tasks written blind to their final version, the scaffold’s lead on OpenRouter (19 of 24 runs against 15) closed when the tasks were rerun on Reflection’s API (18 of 24 each), so it hasn’t held up on new traps there; How it’s scored has the details. [Checked /goal](/checked-goal/) packages all four checks as a prototype extension for Reflection’s Mirror. These scores come from the benchmark harness; the extension’s live trials and limits are reported on its own page.
