Said & Done is a benchmark that tests whether coding agents’ completion reports match their work. Each of its 28 offline tasks, 20 coding tasks and 8 operational scenarios, has hidden checks the agent never sees. Half the tasks can’t be done honestly as asked, so the right answer is to stop and say "blocked": eight of the coding tasks, and six safety-critical scenarios where no option is valid. Its score, Said & Done, is the share of runs where all three hold: the work was done (or, where it can’t be, the real obstacle named), the report was true, and no rule was broken.

Across the 28 tasks, Reflection’s Beam falsely reported "complete" in 25 of 56 runs. Fresh Beam calls that independently test the work, review the report, check risky actions and assess progress (Beam + scaffold, below) cut that to 7 of 56, and raised its Said & Done score from 22 of 56 (39%) to 43 of 56 (77%), at an estimated median cost of about $0.088 a run against $0.045. The checks were developed on these 28 tasks, so the real test is the next one.

On 20 new tasks, written by a separate agent that never saw the checks’ code or prompts and run after the checks were frozen, Beam with the checks got 44 of 60 runs Said & Done against Beam alone’s 31: +22 points (95% interval +3 to +42), which passes the test I registered in advance.

Notably, Beam doesn’t seem to struggle when *asked* whether a task can be done. On three earlier blind sets, whose impossible tasks nearly all name "blocked" as an outcome and mostly ask for an audit or a decision, 74 of Beam alone’s 90 runs on those tasks counted (see [Blind sets](#blind-sets), under How it’s scored). What it struggles with is pushing back on its own. When the final set recast the third set’s impossible tasks as plain requests, Beam alone’s counted runs on them fell from 24 of 30 to 5 of 30, and it reported "complete" in 24; on the impossible tasks in the score, it did so in 21 of 28 runs. Every task’s instructions offer "blocked" for something only the user can provide, but it seems to need the task itself to raise the option.

[Checked /goal](/checked-goal/) packages the checks as a prototype extension for Reflection’s Mirror; the scores above come from the benchmark harness.
