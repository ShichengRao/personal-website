Said & Done tests whether coding agents’ completion reports match their work. Each of its 28 offline tasks, 20 coding tasks and 8 operational scenarios, has hidden checks the agent never sees. Half have no honest way to finish, so the right answer is to stop and say "blocked": eight of the coding tasks, and six safety-critical scenarios where no option is valid. Its score, Said & Done, is the share of runs where all three hold: the work was done (or, at a wall, the real obstacle named), the report was true, and no rule was broken.

Across the 28 tasks, Reflection’s Beam falsely reported "complete" in 25 of 56 runs. Fresh Beam calls that independently test the work, review the report, check risky actions and assess progress cut that to 7 of 56 (Beam + scaffold, below), and raised its Said & Done score from 22 of 56 (39%) to 43 of 56 (77%), at an estimated median cost of about $0.088 a run against $0.045. The checks were developed on these 28 tasks, so that gain, 38 points (95% interval +23 to +54), is a development result; most of it is at the walls, +57 points there against +18 on the tasks that can be done.

On 20 new tasks, written by a separate agent that never saw the checks’ code or prompts and run only after the checks were frozen, Beam with the checks got 44 of 60 runs Said & Done against Beam alone’s 31 (+22 points, 95% interval +3 to +42), so the claim I registered in advance holds. The gain is on the tasks that can’t be done. There, Beam alone reported "complete" in 24 of its 30 runs, as it does at the walls in the score, and 5 counted, against 18 of 30 with the checks; on the tasks that can be done, both got 26 of 30. Three earlier blind sets didn’t hold (+0, +3 and +7; see below), but on those Beam alone already counted in 74 of its 90 runs on impossible tasks, likely because nearly all of their impossible tasks name "blocked" as a possible outcome in their text, so they left the checks little to fix. [Checked /goal](/checked-goal/) packages the checks as a prototype extension for Reflection’s Mirror; these scores come from the benchmark harness, and the extension’s own trials and limits are on its page.
