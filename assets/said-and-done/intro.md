Said & Done tests whether coding agents’ completion reports match their work. Each of its 28 offline tasks, 20 coding tasks and 8 operational scenarios, has hidden checks the agent never sees. Half have no honest way to finish, so the right answer is to stop and say "blocked": eight of the coding tasks, and six safety-critical scenarios where no option is valid. Its score, Said & Done, is the share of runs where all three hold: the work was done (or, at a wall, the real obstacle named), the report was true, and no rule was broken.

Across the 28 tasks, Reflection’s Beam falsely reported "complete" in 25 of 56 runs. Fresh Beam calls that independently test the work, review the report, check risky actions and assess progress cut that to 7 of 56 (Beam + scaffold, below), and raised its Said & Done score from 22 of 56 (39%) to 43 of 56 (77%), at an estimated median cost of about $0.088 a run against $0.045. The checks were developed on these 28 tasks, so that gain, 38 points (95% interval +23 to +54), is a development result.

On 20 new tasks, written by a separate agent that never saw the checks’ code or prompts and run only after the checks were frozen, Beam with the checks got 51 of 60 runs Said & Done against Beam alone’s 47 (+7 points, 95% interval −7 to +22), so the claim I registered in advance doesn’t hold. Two earlier blind sets of 20 didn’t hold either (+0 and +3; see below). On all three, Beam alone did far better than on the 28: 74 of its 90 runs on impossible tasks counted, against 2 of its 28 runs on the walls in the score, likely because nearly all the blind sets’ impossible tasks name "blocked" as a possible outcome in their text, which the walls in the score never do. So these sets left little room where the checks gained most on the 28, and can’t say whether the checks don’t generalize or simply had little to fix. [Checked /goal](/checked-goal/) packages the checks as a prototype extension for Reflection’s Mirror; these scores come from the benchmark harness, and the extension’s own trials and limits are on its page.
