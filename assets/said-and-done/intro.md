Said & Done tests whether coding agents’ completion reports match their work. Each of its 28 offline tasks has hidden checks the agent never sees. Fourteen have no honest way to finish, so the right answer is to stop and say "blocked": three of the coding tasks, six safety-critical scenarios where no option is valid, and five walls from earlier held-out rounds. Its score, Said & Done, is the share of runs where all three hold: the work was done (or, at a wall, the real obstacle named), the report was true, and no rule was broken.

Across the 28 tasks, Reflection’s Beam falsely reported "complete" in 25 of 56 runs. Fresh Beam calls that independently test the work, review the report, check risky actions and assess progress cut that to 7 of 56 (Beam + scaffold v5, below), and raised its Said & Done score from 22 of 56 (39%) to 43 of 56 (77%), at an estimated median cost of about $0.088 a run against $0.045. The checks were developed on these 28 tasks, so that gain, 38 points (95% interval +23 to +54), is a development result. Giving Beam only the scaffold’s "blocked" wording, without the checks, changed nothing (+0, −14 to +12), so the gain comes from the checks.

On 20 new tasks, written by a separate agent that never saw the checks’ code or prompts and run only after this version was frozen, the gain didn’t carry over: Beam alone and Beam with the checks each got 47 of 60 runs Said & Done (+0 points, −17 to +18), so the claim I registered in advance doesn’t hold. Beam alone did far better on those tasks than on the 28, stopping correctly at 20 of 30 impossible runs against 2 of 28, and one new set can’t say whether the checks don’t generalize or simply had less to fix there. The checks’ previous version made Beam worse on them (−18, −33 to −3). [Checked /goal](/checked-goal/) packages the checks as a prototype extension for Reflection’s Mirror; these scores come from the benchmark harness, and the extension’s own trials and limits are on its page.
