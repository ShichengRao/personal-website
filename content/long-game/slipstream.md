Arcade racers reward the last-millisecond brake and the perfect drift. This one
adds the thing real racing is actually about: pace management. Your tires have a
**temperature** and a **life**. Pushing (extra power) heats them fast; hot tires
slide wide and wear out; worn tires never get their grip back. Running in another
car's **slipstream** gives you a speed boost and cools the tires, and the track is
narrow enough that a car ahead can genuinely **block**.

The field has personalities. The chargers push from the first corner and lead early;
the managers sit in the tow and wait. In a Sprint the chargers usually get away with
it. In an Endurance race the tires decide, and a driver who spent the middle laps
saving rubber arrives at the finish with the grip to pass.

**The slow way.** Stay in the tow, keep the temperature in the green, hold the
inside line when someone lines you up, and let the pushers overheat in front of you.
The timing tower tells you who is fading.

**The fast way.** Hold push on every straight, brake late, and build a gap while the
tires are fresh. It works if you can manage a car that slides more every lap.
